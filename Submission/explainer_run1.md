**Multi‑Agent Workshop (MAW) Overview**

A Multi‑Agent Workshop (MAW) is a short, time‑bounded event that brings together researchers, developers, and domain experts to design, prototype, and evaluate systems composed of multiple autonomous agents that must coordinate to achieve shared objectives. Participants typically represent AI/ML, robotics, economics, cognitive science, and human‑computer interaction, reflecting the interdisciplinary expertise required for algorithmic coordination, incentive design, and user experience. [source needed]

**Iterative Prototyping Cycle**

During the workshop, teams follow a rapid‑iteration loop:  
1. Propose a coordination mechanism (e.g., market‑based bidding, contract‑net negotiation, reinforcement‑learning policy).  
2. Implement a prototype.  
3. Test it in a shared simulation or on real hardware.  
4. Analyse the results and refine the design.  

This cycle repeats over a few days, allowing quick identification of effective approaches. [source needed]

**Open‑Source Artefacts**

Most recent MAWs release all code, datasets, and evaluation scripts publicly—often on GitHub—creating a reusable “Multi‑Agent Coordination Suite.” [source needed]

**Emerging Standards & Metrics**

The community is converging on a core set of performance metrics to enable fair comparison across approaches:  

- **Collective reward** – total payoff achieved by the agent team.  
- **Fairness / egalitarianism** – equitable distribution of rewards among agents.  
- **Communication overhead** – bandwidth or message‑passing cost.  
- **Robustness to agent failure** – performance degradation when agents drop out.  

These metrics are documented in recent workshop proceedings. [source needed]

**Open Research Questions**

- **Scalability:** How do current coordination protocols (decentralized learning, market mechanisms, etc.) perform when scaling from dozens to thousands of heterogeneous agents in real‑world environments, and what new theoretical guarantees are required? [source needed]

- **Human‑Agent Governance:** What frameworks or normative guidelines should govern interactions between autonomous agents and human stakeholders to ensure transparency, accountability, and alignment with societal values? [source needed]
